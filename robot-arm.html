7-DOF Quasi-Direct-Drive Collaborative Robot Arm

payload/weight ~1:1 reach 1.11 m mass 10 kg actuators RobStride QDD × 7

A commercial-grade 7-DOF collaborative arm modeled after cobots like the Flexiv Rizon and UR series. Fully 3D-printed structure, decentralized CAN bus motor control, and a peak payload nearly equal to its own weight.

  • Elite payload-to-weight ratio: a 10 kg custom 3D-printed arm rated for a ~10 kg peak payload at full 1.11 m horizontal extension, and a 3–5 kg continuous working payload.
  • Cut ~2 kg of structural mass through slicer parameter optimization — an isotropic 7% gyroid infill matrix maximizes torsional rigidity per gram.
  • Industrial cobot architecture: uniform cylindrical links, spigot structural joints, slip-ring internal routing, and decentralized CAN bus control.
  • Managed a 5,000+ component SolidWorks assembly — SpeedPaks and mass-suppression of 600+ fasteners dropped rebuild times from 10 minutes to seconds.

01Mechanical Design — SolidWorks

▣
[ img: CAD assembly ]
Full isometric SolidWorks render — uniform cylindrical links and joint hubs

Cobot-style uniform links. Uniform cylindrical link diameters instead of legacy tapered arms — power and CAN wiring routes internally through slip rings, base to tool head, with zero external snag points.

Large-assembly management. The 5,000+ component assembly stays responsive through rigid sub-assemblies, geometry defeaturing, and SpeedPaks.

Spigot joints. 400 mm links are printed in segments and joined with interlocking male/female spigot joints with a 1 mm alignment lip. The lip carries transverse shear; epoxy and M3/M4 fasteners with heat-set inserts provide axial clamping.

Result: no joint flex, and no shear load on the fasteners themselves.

Mass optimization. Printed in PETG on an Elegoo Centauri Carbon. Iterative slicer testing settled on 3 wall loops (1.2 mm shell) + 7% gyroid infill — saving ~2 kg across the arm.

Why gyroid? Rectilinear infill is weak against 45° grain loads. Gyroid's 3D isotropic matrix resists the torsional moments generated by the QDD shoulder actuators in every direction.

With inverse kinematics keeping the elbow slightly flexed (shorter moment arm), the arm sustains continuous industrial payloads of 3–5 kg without exceeding the motor’s 40 Nm continuous thermal rating — on par with commercial cobots.

03Electrical Architecture — CAN Bus

⏚
[ img: internal wiring ]
CAN + power umbilical routed through the center of the cylindrical joints

Decentralized CAN bus. An ESP32 commands the RobStride actuators (RS04 / RS03 / RS02 / RS00) over differential CAN (CAN-H / CAN-L) — high noise immunity against brushless motor EMI.

Power/data isolation. 24V/48V main power shares the umbilical with CAN data, but stays strictly isolated from logic-level transceiver lines — a transient spike never reaches the MCU.

⌾
[ img: robstride actuator ]
Close-up of a RobStride QDD actuator mounted in a joint hub

Quasi-direct-drive: low gear ratio → high backdrivability and torque transparency, the same actuation approach as modern force-controlled cobots.

Single controller, seven axes — CAN's multi-drop topology means one twisted pair daisy-chains through every joint.

04Embedded Control — C++ / ESP32

Each RobStride motor takes an 8-byte CAN frame packing target position, velocity, stiffness (KP), damping (KD), and feed-forward torque — floats scaled into fixed-width integers and bit-packed into the payload.

can_motor_control.cpp

// Pack float targets into an 8-byte CAN frame for RobStride QDD actuators
#include <ESP32-TWAI-CAN.hpp>

void sendMotorCommand(uint16_t motorID, float pos, float vel,
                      float kp, float kd, float torque) {
  CanFrame frame = { 0 };
  frame.identifier       = motorID;   // standard IDs 0x01–0x07
  frame.extd             = 0;
  frame.data_length_code = 8;

  // Scale floats into fixed-width ints based on motor limits
  uint16_t pos_int = float_to_uint(pos, P_MIN, P_MAX, 16);
  uint16_t vel_int = float_to_uint(vel, V_MIN, V_MAX, 12);
  uint16_t kp_int  = float_to_uint(kp, KP_MIN, KP_MAX, 12);

  // Bit-pack into the payload
  frame.data[0] = pos_int >> 8;
  frame.data[1] = pos_int & 0xFF;
  frame.data[2] = vel_int >> 4;
  frame.data[3] = ((vel_int & 0xF) << 4) | (kp_int >> 8);
  // ... bytes 4–7 pack KD and feed-forward torque

  ESP32Can.writeFrame(frame);         // transmit over CAN-H / CAN-L
}
