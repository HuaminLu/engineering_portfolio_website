Balsa Wood Truss Bridge — Mechanics of Deformable Solids

course MTE 219 (MODS) institution University of Waterloo simulation SolidWorks FEA analysis Method of Joints & Sections

Course culminating design and structural optimization project for MTE 219: Mechanics of Deformable Solids at the University of Waterloo. Engineered an ultra-lightweight balsa wood truss bridge designed to maximize payload-to-weight structural efficiency under center-point and distributed deck loading. Combined analytical method-of-joints force calculations with SolidWorks FEA simulations using customized orthotropic balsa material properties to resolve axial tensions, compressions, and Euler buckling limits across each link.

🌉
[ img: balsa truss bridge — full assembly & load test ]
Full-span balsa wood truss bridge mounted on hydraulic test fixture undergoing ultimate payload testing
  • Analytical Truss Force Analysis: Performed comprehensive static equilibrium, Method of Joints, and Method of Sections calculations to resolve exact axial loads (tension vs. compression) across every individual structural link under varying payload distributions.
  • SolidWorks FEA Simulation: Configured finite element models with directional orthotropic wood material properties (longitudinal vs. transverse elastic modulus, shear modulus, and Poisson's ratios) assigned to individual members to accurately simulate balsa grain mechanics.
  • Cross-Section & Buckling Optimization: Iterated link dimensions across high-load paths — reinforced compressive top chords against critical Euler buckling while slimming pure tension members to minimize deadweight.
  • Gusset Joint Shear Design: Engineered double-lap balsa gusset joints with optimized contact bond area, ensuring failure occurred through structural member limits rather than premature adhesive joint shear peeling.
